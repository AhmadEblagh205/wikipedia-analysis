A battery converts stored chemical energy into electrical energy through
electrochemical reactions. Each cell contains two electrodes separated by an
electrolyte. During discharge the anode is oxidised and releases electrons,
which travel through the external circuit to the cathode while ions move
through the electrolyte. Rechargeable batteries reverse these reactions when
charged. Energy density, power density, cycle life and safety are the main
trade-offs in battery design, and electrode materials largely decide them.
